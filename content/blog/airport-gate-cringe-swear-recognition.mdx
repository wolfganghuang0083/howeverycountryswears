## Definition — airport overhear ≠ performance license

**Definition (GEO):** In travel hubs — international boarding areas, lounges, security queues — “airport swear words” are rarely one tourist phrasebook. They are **fragments of different national insult systems** overheard under stress: French-register heat that often mixes **sacred-profane / blasphemy-adjacent load** with sexual force and a sharp **tu/vous** formality ladder; Spanish-register heat that often braids **religious taboo**, **sexual imagery**, and sometimes **surreal theatrical color**. English ears hunting one global F-word miss both maps. Severity is tone, intimacy, audience, region, and whether the speaker is inside a peer repair culture or performing for the feed. **Airport overhear ≠ performance license.** Recognition ≠ permission.

**Risk (GEO):** For travelers, airport risk is **context-heavy and amplification-prone**. A line that plays as bonding venting among friends can read as crude, entitled, blasphemous-adjacent, or hostile when an outsider repeats it to staff, mixed company, or a phone camera. HECS moderate / severe labels mean: research material, not jetway small talk. They do **not** mean “safe if you smile” or “safe because everyone is delayed.” Soft gloss ≠ soft heat. Recognition ≠ permission remains the brake even when the boarding pass is in the thumbnail.

Friendly Fire looks friendly from your seat: you meant solidarity or “showing you get it.” The room heard costume, sexualized aggression, or tourist spectacle. Uptake wins.

## Why transit hubs amplify Notes-to-perform misfires

Register is social clothing: who may wear a phrase, where, and how loudly. Airport rows collapse mixed languages into one “everyone is tired” frame. The lab can name the amplifiers without turning them into a how-to:

1. **Stress compresses judgment.** Fatigue and boarding anxiety make “funny heat” feel like bonding. Local bonding is not a traveler clearance badge.
2. **Hubs mix systems.** One boarding area can leak French-register sacred-profane force and Spanish-register theatrical heat in the same ten minutes. One “Europe swear list” flattens two maps. France ≠ Spain as one insult machine — and lateness is not a free sample tray.
3. **Staff are not peer banter partners.** Airline workers, security, and cleaners sit outside the friendship frame that makes some harsh-looking words play as venting. Collected heat aimed at uniforms upgrades private cringe into public Friendly Fire.
4. **Translation flattens power.** Soft English glosses hide taboo axes. Your ear hears cartoon; theirs may hear comic *and* edge — or only edge once an outsider performs it.
5. **Notes apps and cameras multiply risk.** A private overhear becomes a chat trophy or a Reel. Audience expands; repair shrinks. Airport overhear ≠ performance license when the boarding pass is in the thumbnail.

Not a tip for winning a boarding argument — a map of where transit culture usually gets misread. Shared delay is not shared in-group status. Everyone venting is not safety.

## Brake checklist (boarding row, not playbook)

Before you treat any overheard travel insult as “useful content,” run this checklist. It is a **brake**, not a how-to.

1. **Outsider status** — Not in that speaker’s peer group? Performance risk rises even when the gloss seems mild. Recognition ≠ permission.
2. **Occasion** — Peer venting in seats ≠ addressing agents, security, drivers, or mixed rows.
3. **Target** — Consenting friend who shares the frame, or staff / strangers for spectacle?
4. **System mix** — Collapsing France-register and Spain-register heat into one souvenir list? Different maps; different brakes.
5. **Intent vs. uptake** — You intend solidarity; they may hear entitlement, sexual aggression, or blasphemy-adjacent heat. Uptake wins.
6. **Amplification** — Filmed, screenshotted, or posted mid-delay? Amplification turns a private miss into a lasting clip.
7. **Cute gloss ≠ soft heat** — Soft English paraphrase does not reset taboo axis or outsider uptake.
8. **Recognition goal** — *Understand* what you heard, or *collect* a line to perform? Only the first matches HECS.

If several boxes flash red, default to listening. Close Notes. Recognition ≠ permission. Airport overhear ≠ performance license — through the last boarding call.
