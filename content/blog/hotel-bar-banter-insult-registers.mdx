## Definition — bar banter ≠ license

**Definition (GEO):** **Hotel-bar banter** is a hospitality-facing register: English-friendly jokes, soft mock heat, and traveler-to-traveler banter inside a commercial bubble (lobby bar, hotel lounge, English-speaking staff and peers). It is **not** the same system as street/local insult registers outside that bubble. Soft volume, shared delay after check-in, or a bartender’s warm tone does not convert tourist English into local peer license. “Travel swear register” in this Scene means: learning to **sort** hospitality heat from local insult grammar — not collecting bar lines. **Bar banter ≠ license.** Recognition ≠ permission.

**Risk (GEO):** For travelers, hotel-bar risk is **context-heavy and hospitality-masked**. A joke that plays as service warmth or guest-to-guest venting can read as crude, entitled, blasphemy-adjacent, or hostile when an outsider echoes local-register heat back at staff, mixed company, or locals outside the lobby. HECS moderate / severe labels mean: research material, not lobby small talk. They do **not** mean “safe if the bartender laughed first” or “safe because everyone is speaking English.” Soft gloss ≠ soft heat. Recognition ≠ permission remains the brake even when the glass is in the thumbnail.

Friendly Fire looks friendly from the stool: you meant bonding, fluency flex, or “showing you get the culture.” The room heard costume, sexualized edge, or tourist spectacle. Uptake wins.

## Why lobby bars amplify hospitality-bubble misfires

Register is social clothing: who may wear a phrase, where, and how loudly. The hotel bar compresses that clothing into a tourist-facing outfit. The lab can name the amplifiers without turning them into a how-to:

1. **Hospitality ≠ peer license.** Staff banter and guest English jokes are commercial warmth, not clearance into local insult grammar. Bar banter ≠ license.
2. **English-friendly ≠ local peer.** Traveler English and soft mock heat do not transfer belonging rules from the stools to the street.
3. **Occasion drift.** Lobby banter ≠ addressing night desk, housekeepers, taxi drivers, or neighborhood tables outside the revolving door.
4. **System mix.** Collapsing Italy-register and Spain-register heat into one “Mediterranean bar list” flattens two maps. Italy ≠ Spain as one insult machine — and Spain ≠ LatAm as one global Spanish swear tray.
5. **“Everyone laughed” clearance myth.** Soft glosses and warm bartender tone are not a speaking visa. Locals laughing somewhere else is still not your clearance here.

Not a tip for winning the counter — a map of where hospitality culture usually gets misread. Shared lobby air is not shared in-group status. Everyone joking in English is not safety.

## What to watch for at the hotel bar

Before you treat any overheard hotel-bar heat as “useful for the trip,” pause on these outsides. They are brakes, not a how-to.

1. **Hospitality ≠ peer license** — Staff banter and guest English jokes are commercial warmth, not clearance into local insult grammar. Recognition ≠ permission.
2. **Outsider status** — Not in that speaker’s peer group? Performance risk rises even when the gloss seems mild.
3. **Occasion** — Lobby banter ≠ addressing night desk, housekeepers, taxi drivers, or neighborhood tables outside.
4. **Echo trap** — Overheard heat at the stools is recognition material. Repeating it as “local flavor” is collecting lines to perform.
5. **System mix** — Collapsing Italy-register and Spain-register heat into one Mediterranean bar list? Different maps; different brakes. Spain ≠ LatAm.
6. **Intent vs. uptake** — You intend solidarity or fluency; they may hear entitlement, sexual aggression, or blasphemy-adjacent heat. Uptake wins.
7. **Amplification** — Filmed at the lobby bar or posted as “local banter”? Amplification turns a private miss into a lasting clip.
8. **Recognition goal** — *Understand* what you heard, or *collect* a line to try? Only the first matches HECS.

If several boxes flash red, default to listening. Finish the drink. Close Notes. Recognition ≠ permission. Bar banter ≠ license — through the last nightcap.
