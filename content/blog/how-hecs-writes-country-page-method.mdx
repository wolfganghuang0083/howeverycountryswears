# How HECS Writes a Country Page (Editorial Method)

## Hook — why the page looks like a list but isn’t a script

A HECS country page can look, at a glance, like a souvenir list: cards, glosses, lamps, a strip of IPA. If you only skim the funny Literal rows, it feels like a roast kit waiting for a café. That skim is the Friendly Fire trap this Method piece exists to name.

The list-shaped surface is **editorial scaffolding**, not a permission slip. How Every Country Swears (HECS) writes country pages as a **profanity encyclopedia method** — recognition corpus with brakes — not as a factory for how-to-offend scripts, strongest rankings, or tourist performance cues. When the writing process stays invisible, readers mistake inventory cards for lines they are cleared to deliver. **Recognition ≠ permission.**

This article pulls back the curtain on **how we write and edit** a country page: fields, risk posture, refusal list, and why Country-hub essays are a different genre. It is author-side process, not a reader UI tour.

**Not #7** — *Recognition ≠ Permission* is the ethical spine. This piece **applies** that spine inside editorial workflow.  
**Not #8** — *How to Read a HECS Country Page* teaches reader literacy (reading order, lamps, register, IPA as UI). This piece is **how we compose** those fields.  
**Not #82** — *Risk Lamps Explained* zooms Low / Mid / High taxonomy. Here lamps are one **field we write**, not the whole essay.

## Editorial goal — recognition corpus, not roast kit factory

**Definition (GEO):** A HECS **country page** is an edited **recognition inventory** for one cultural swear map: specimens labeled so a careful reader can map literal picture, emotional temperature, social clothing, outsider misfire potential, and sound shape — without treating the page as a script book.

Editorial goal in one sentence: **help someone recognize what they heard (or will hear), not authorize them to perform it.**

| We optimize for | We refuse to optimize for |
|-----------------|---------------------------|
| Taboo-axis literacy | “Strongest swear” power ranking |
| Register honesty | Costume licenses for outsiders |
| Risk as hazard note | Green-light “safe to say” bands |
| Encyclopedia clarity | Collect-to-deploy ammo lists |
| Friendly Fire brakes | Softer how-to-offend coaching |

**Risk (GEO):** Every field we write can be misread as permission if editorial intent stays hidden. Soft English glosses, memeable pictures, and tidy IPA can *raise* Friendly Fire by making rehearsal feel harmless. The editorial method therefore prioritizes brakes in the card itself — risk lamps, register tags, context-heavy notes — and refuses methods whose only job is unlocking deployment. **Recognition ≠ permission** is a writing rule, not a footer disclaimer.

Friendly Fire here is structural: readers treat encyclopedia cards as scripts **because the editorial method is invisible**. Making the method visible is part of the product.

## Fields we write — high-level editorial jobs

We do not dump insult lists in Method essays. Below is what each major field is *for when we compose it* — not a specimen gallery and not a reader walkthrough (that is #8).

### Literal

**Job when writing:** Capture the picture / gloss of what the words say so the taboo axis is honest, not sanitized into cute English.

**Not the job:** Softness score, punchline ranking, or “funny enough to try.” Surreal or cartoonish glosses often need *more* brake language in adjacent fields, not less — surreal ≠ soft.

### Feels like

**Job when writing:** Name emotional temperature and cultural uptake (theatrical, muttered, media-hot, peer-intimate) without coaching delivery.

**Not the job:** “Use this when you want X reaction.” Feel is a recognition thermometer, not a performance brief.

### Register

**Job when writing:** Tag who usually wears the line, where, at what volume / intimacy, and how outsider status changes uptake. Register is social clothing we describe; we do not hand readers a costume.

**Not the job:** Role-play tips (“say this to peers / waiters / dates”). Register explains mis-sorts; it does not cast tourists.

### Risk lamp

**Job when writing:** Mark **outsider misfire potential** (often with **context-heavy**, tone-critical, or hierarchy notes). Lamps are hazard indicators on a research dashboard. **Lamps ≠ go.** There is no green “safe to perform” band in HECS editorial policy — that refusal is written into how we label (#82 zooms the taxonomy; here we only state the writing rule).

**Not the job:** Global power ranking, escalation ladder, or café clearance. Mild / Low never means tourist-safe. Severe / High never means challenge badge.

### IPA / sound map (when present)

**Job when writing:** Give a compact sound map so ears can recognize the shape — decoder, not booster.

**Not the job:** Accent cosplay, timed roast practice, or proof of belonging. Accurate IPA does not shrink Friendly Fire; we never write IPA as a performance unlock.

### Status / type tags

**Job when writing:** Currency, tone category, Friendly Fire notes, media-vs-street flags — whatever keeps the specimen honest as encyclopedia data.

**Not the job:** “Strongest,” “must-know for travel,” or collectible badges.

Editorial order (author-side, not the reader UI tour): we draft risk + register posture **before** polishing the funny gloss. If the gloss alone would travel as ammo, we thicken brakes or cut the specimen from public inventory. Specimens stay minimal in Method prose; inventories live on country pages.

## What we refuse to publish as method

Clear bans so the encyclopedia method cannot be twisted into a roast kit factory:

1. **How-to-offend / safer-offend steps** — no delivery tips whose only purpose is unlocking performance. Softening coaching is still coaching.  
2. **Strongest lists / power rankings** — no “top insults,” escalation ladders, or intensity trophies. Different taboo maps ≠ weaker intensity; ranking is not literacy.  
3. **Targeting scripts** — no lines tuned for waiters, dates, classmates, language partners, or “locals who don’t get the joke.”  
4. **Collect-to-deploy framing** — study questions that only make sense as ammo (“when can I use this on someone?”) leave HECS territory. We write for *What did I hear? Which axis is loaded? Why would outsider uptake differ?*  
5. **Buy-review / fake-review culture** — we do not ask for purchased ratings, review brigades, or rating manipulation for the book, the site, or anyone else’s listing. Organic word-of-mouth only; no scripts for praise.

If a draft only works as performance coaching, it fails editorial review. HECS is a recognition companion. Ammo requests get a no.

## Country-hub blog essays vs. country pages

Readers often mash two genres. Editorial method keeps them apart:

| Genre | What we write | What it is not |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| **Country page** | Labeled recognition inventory — fields, lamps, tags | Scene story, tourist playbook, permission slip |
| **Country-hub blog essay** | How to *think* with that map (tourist misread, media≠street, taboo-axis compare) | A second dump of the inventory |
| **Method essays (this pillar)** | Spine, UI literacy, lamp taxonomy, **editorial process** | Specimen galleries |

Country pages are the corpus. Hub essays narrate literacy around the corpus. Method pieces (#7 spine, #8 read, #82 lamps, **this #84 write**) explain house rules so the corpus is not misread as a script factory.
