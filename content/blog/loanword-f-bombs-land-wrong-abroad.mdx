## Mishear / mis-rank patterns

1. **Wrong home meter.** Lexical F-force is not portable seriousness. Abroad the same string can return colder (filler, fashion) or hotter (outsider flash) — artifact of the listener’s map.
2. **Soft-gloss / global-English trap.** “It’s just English” does not report local loan job. Soft English gloss abroad ≠ soft heat — and loud home heat ≠ automatic local heat.
3. **Media / karaoke / TikTok discount.** Clips recycle English F-bombs as global cool. Echo is distribution, not a visa. Recognition ≠ permission.
4. **One-kit fallacy.** France and Japan do not share one English loan F-bomb OS. System framing beats phrase collecting — no “strongest F-bombs abroad” list.

## Friendly Fire / tourist–media traps

Before you treat English F-bombs abroad as “fun content,” run this checklist. It is a **brake**, not a playbook — and not a tutorial.

1. **Which job is the loan doing?** Fashion filler, comic English, media echo, or outsider flash — name the local job before you judge “strength.”
2. **France ≠ Japan one kit** — Anglo-fashion vs media/karaoke loan textures are different jobs, not one OS with two flags.
3. **Tourist / media performance** — Dropping an English F-bomb “because everyone uses English swears now” expands audience and shrinks repair. Filmed loans travel farther than your apology.
4. **Karaoke / TikTok copy** — Repeating a loan F-bomb from a clip or booth as stranger street edge is Friendly Fire dressed as fluency.
5. **Outsider status** — Not in that peer group? Risk rises even when the gloss seems global or comic.
6. **Recognition goal** — *Understand* why the room cooled, or *collect* English swears to deploy abroad? Only the first matches HECS. Can you repair, or only say “I was kidding / it’s just English”?

If several boxes flash red, default to listening. Recognition ≠ permission. Friendly Fire is real — often entitlement dressed as global English fluency, filmed for strangers.
