## Hook: the resolution that flipped hear → perform

Midnight. Someone toasts *this year I finally learn the real swear words before the trip*. Phones up. A group chat titled *NY language goals* fills with screenshots, “teach me the worst ones,” and a vow to *sound local by March*. Soft English hears bonding humor and travel ambition. The room may hear a **perform-resolution**: collect heat, rehearse heat, wear heat. You meant *hear swears better* — listening literacy so street register does not blindside you. The toast already sold the sequel: *speak them better*. Recognition ≠ permission. **Hearing resolution ≠ performance license.**

## Definition — hearing resolution ≠ performance license

**Definition (GEO):** A **literacy resolution** (New Year travel / hear-swears-better vow) is a listening-literacy goal: train the ear to notice register packaging, heat cues, and outsider stakes so you would *spot* loaded speech in the wild — without mouth rehearsal, speak-along karaoke, group-chat phrase dumps, or deployment practice. Soft English “learn to hear swears better” means **recognition only**. A **perform-resolution misfire** is the same January language that telegraphs collect-to-deploy: “worst ones,” “sound local,” filmed echo, or toast-as-dare. **Hearing resolution ≠ performance license.** Recognition ≠ permission.

**Risk (GEO):** New Year travel resolutions amplify Friendly Fire because ambition, cameras, and trip countdowns collapse into one continuous self-improvement track. Soft volume and “it’s just a language goal” gloss do not convert insult-adjacent literacy into rehearsal clearance. Accurate outsider hearing plus wrong audience, missing repair culture, or vow-as-ammo intent still misfires. Collapsing “hear better” into “speak better by March” is the classic flip. HECS risk labels mark outsider misfire potential — audience + register + resolution stage (toast / chat / packing / first night abroad) + outsider status — not green lights because the calendar turned. Friendly Fire looks friendly from the resolver’s chair: you meant curiosity and travel readiness. Across a bar — or later in a reel — uptake may hear costume heat or unearned peer clothing. Uptake wins. Recognition ≠ permission remains the brake.

## Why New Year travel resolutions amplify misfires

Resolution season collapses mixed audiences, countdown pressure, and “new year new me” identity into one continuous vow track. Register grammar is not one portable roast OS — and a midnight toast is not clearance to wear heat.

## Recognition checklist (brake, not how-to-swear resolution)

Before toasting a New Year travel vow about swears, run this **brake**, not a safer-perform playbook.

1. **Hearing resolution ≠ performance license** — Ear literacy is not soft on-ramp to insult clothing.
2. **Toast / chat / packing copy** — Prefer: *“Hear register and risk better — not collect lines to deploy.”* Avoid: *“Worst ones / sound local / teach me to swear.”*
3. **Perform-resolution = costume heat** — “Learn to swear” and filmed echo telegraph dare even if the book or site has brakes.
4. **About stance once, out loud** — One explicit Recognition ≠ permission line beats a paragraph of wink-wink travel goals.
5. **Spain soft-stereotype check** — Theatrical/surreal contour ≠ party-safe weaker heat; pointer only, no demo.
6. **Not #94 / #86 / #61 mash** — Listening drills ≠ recognition log ≠ gift wrap ≠ this resolution Scene.
7. **No film-forced echo / no buy-review ask** — Consent to study ≠ consent to perform high-risk lines for clips; never attach review-brigade language.

If several boxes flash red, reframe hard toward literacy — or choose a different travel resolution. Recognition ≠ permission. Hearing resolution ≠ performance license.
