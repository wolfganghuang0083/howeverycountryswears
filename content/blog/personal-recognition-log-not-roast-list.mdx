# Building a Personal Recognition Log (Without a Roast List)

## Hook — recognition log vs. roast-list temptation

You hear something hot on a train, in a clip, or at a café table two seats over. Your hand goes to a notes app. That impulse can be literacy — *what did I just map?* — or it can be ammo collection — *what can I perform later?* The notebook looks the same either way. The ethics do not.

How Every Country Swears (HECS) builds recognition tools: taboo maps, risk labels, register notes. A **personal recognition log** is the private twin of that method — a hearing journal that stops at understanding. A **roast list** is the same ink turned into a phrase dump, practice script, or collectible insult kit. **Log ≠ script. Recognition ≠ permission.**

This Method piece is about **keeping a personal hearing/recognition log without turning it into roast ammo**. It assumes the ethical spine (#7), country-page reading (#8), risk-lamp literacy (#82), outsider-accent stakes (#83), how we write country pages (#84), and phrase-card field jobs (#85). The zoom here is **swear journal ethics**: what you write down, why you stop, and how Friendly Fire starts in the notebook long before it hits a room.

## What a recognition log is (and is not)

**Definition (GEO):** A **personal recognition log** is a private hearing journal for mapping overheard or studied heat — date, map/context, register note, risk label, and an explicit stop line — so you remember *what you recognized*, not so you stock lines to deploy. It is **recognition log language**: system and ethics labels for your ear, not a swear journal built to rehearse.

| Recognition log is | Recognition log is not |
|--------------------|------------------------|
| A hearing / literacy journal | A roast list or phrase dump |
| Map tiles: context, register, risk | A practice script or delivery drill |
| “I stop here” as a field | Collect-to-deploy inventory |
| Private study posture | Content farm of overheard heat |
| Companion to HECS brakes | Souvenir insult kit |

**Risk (GEO):** The ethical risk of a swear journal is **collect-to-deploy drift** — treating overheard heat as collectibles, sharing the log as content, or converting a country taboo map (for example an illness-axis inventory) into a souvenir script. A log that never names a stop line tends to become a roast list. **Recognition ≠ permission** remains the brake even when the notebook is private.

## Risk / ethics of logging overheard heat

Logging is not neutral. What you write shapes what you rehearse in your head. HECS cares about three journal-side Friendly Fire patterns:

1. **Collectibles mindset** — Entries become trophies: funny glosses, “got one,” denser pages = stronger traveler identity. Heat is filed as merchandise, not as a map tile with stakes.  
2. **Log-as-content** — Quoting overheard lines for engagement (reels, group chats, “listen what I heard”) turns private recognition into performance with an audience. Posting is performance; amplification shrinks repair.  
3. **Map-as-souvenir-script** — Reading a country inventory — say the Netherlands illness-axis map — and copying axis labels or field notes as if they were lines to try. The inventory is for **reading risk into your log**, not for stocking NL roast ammo.

**Study posture (allowed):** *What taboo axis seemed loaded? What register did the room seem to use? What risk label would I assign before I pretend I belong?*  
**Left HECS (banned here):** how-to swear, strongest lists, buy-review asks, power rankings, collect-to-deploy journals, “daily roast practice,” or hard-coded phrase URLs for rehearsal.

Intent does not clear the notebook. Warm curiosity plus a growing phrase dump is still collect-to-deploy. **Recognition ≠ permission.**

## Field ideas for a log — system labels, not specimen dumps

Keep fields **thin and ethical**. Specimens stay minimal: if you must note a gloss, note it as a *picture you heard*, then stop. Do not build pronunciation drills, escalation ladders, or “use when” columns.

Suggested recognition-log fields (ethics labels, not insult kits):

| Field | Job | Refuse |
|-------|-----|--------|
| **Date / place-type** | Situate the hearing (train, clip, classroom) | Exact targeting of named strangers as roast subjects |
| **Map** | Which culture / inventory you are reading against | Treating the map as a souvenir script |
| **Register note** | Mutters, theater, peer banter, media heat — what *kind* of uptake you think you heard | Costume direction for your own delivery |
| **Risk label** | Outsider misfire note (Low/Mid/High or context-heavy) copied as a **brake** | Green light / “safe enough to try” |
| **“I stop here”** | Explicit end of the entry: recognition complete | Practice reps, timing tips, camera takes |

Optional literacy pointers (names only): if you use HECS card language, you may jot that Literal was soft while Risk still felt Mid — that is **field literacy** (#85), not clearance. Soft gloss ≠ soft heat. Lamps ≠ go (#82).

## Friendly Fire / brake checklist

Before you close an entry, run the brakes:

- [ ] Did I write to **understand** what I heard — or to **stock a line**?  
- [ ] Is there an **“I stop here”** field, or only more glosses?  
- [ ] Would sharing this entry be **explanation of register** — or **performance of heat**?  
- [ ] Am I using a country map (e.g. NL illness-axis) as **risk literacy** — or as **souvenir ammo**?  
- [ ] Does any column look like **daily roast practice**, timing tips, or “say it to a native”?  

If two or more answers lean deploy, delete the rehearsal columns. Keep the map tiles. A thinner log is often the ethical one. Stance and authorship live on [About](https://howeverycountryswears.com/about?utm_source=hecs_blog&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=recognition_log).
