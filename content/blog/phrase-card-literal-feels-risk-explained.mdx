# From Phrase Card to Culture: What Literal / Feels Like / Risk Mean

## Hook — three labels, one culture map

Open a HECS phrase card and three labels sit near the top: **Literal**, **Feels Like**, **Risk**. Your eye often hits Literal first — the picture is funny, surreal, or oddly soft in English — and your brain files the whole card as “cute enough to try.” That skim is the Friendly Fire trap this Method piece exists to name.

Those three fields are **recognition literacy layers**. Read together, they turn a card into a **culture map**: what the words paint, what temperature the culture usually assigns, and how easily an outsider misfires. Read Literal alone, and the card collapses into a souvenir caption — soft gloss mistaken for soft heat, soft heat mistaken for clearance.

How Every Country Swears (HECS) builds phrase cards so you can recognize what you heard, not rehearse delivery. **Soft gloss ≠ soft heat. Recognition ≠ permission.**

This article zooms **reader literacy of card anatomy** — Literal / Feels Like / Risk meanings — not the ethical spine (#7), full country-page UI tour (#8), Low/Mid/High taxonomy alone (#82), or how we *write* fields (#84).

## Literal — picture without permission

**Definition (GEO):** On a HECS **phrase card**, **Literal** is the picture / gloss of what the words *say* — the image or paraphrase that makes the taboo axis visible. Its job is honesty about surface meaning so you can map *what was painted*, not treat the gloss as a softness score.

| Literal is | Literal is not |
|------------|----------------|
| A picture / gloss of the words | A softness meter |
| Taboo-axis visibility | Joke license or café clearance |
| Recognition scaffolding | “Funny enough to try on strangers” |
| One layer of a culture map | The whole culture map |

**Friendly Fire (classic):** reading **Literal only** and treating a soft or memeable English gloss as clearance. Cartoonish pictures travel well; surreal images feel harmless. Mild-looking glosses under-report sexual, religious, honor, illness, or hierarchy heat that Feels Like and Risk still carry. **Soft gloss ≠ soft heat.**

Use Literal as a decoder: *What picture am I hearing?* Stop before *May I perform this picture?* A soft English caption can *raise* Friendly Fire by making rehearsal feel safe. The field is a map tile, not a permission slip. **Recognition ≠ permission.**

## Feels Like — temperature, not script cue

**Feels Like** names **emotional temperature and cultural uptake** — theatrical, muttered, peer-intimate, media-hot, comic, blistering — without coaching *when* or *at whom* to deliver the line.

| Feels Like is | Feels Like is not |
|---------------|-------------------|
| A recognition thermometer | A performance brief |
| How the culture often *takes* the heat | “Use this when you want X reaction” |
| Temperature relative to that map | Global “strength” ranking |
| Context for the ear | Costume direction for outsiders |

Temperature is not intensity-trophy language. “Feels theatrical” does not mean tourist-safe theater. “Feels muted among peers” does not mean green for café banter abroad. Feel explains why a soft Literal can still land hard — or why a nuclear-looking picture can sit in peer banter *inside* a repair culture you do not inhabit.

Do not read Feels Like as a script cue. Script cues ask *When do I say this to get a laugh?* Thermometers ask *What heat am I hearing?* The first leaves HECS; the second stays literacy. **Recognition ≠ permission.**

## Risk — outsider misfire, not intensity trophy

**Risk (GEO):** On the phrase card, **Risk** (the risk lamp / risk label) marks **outsider misfire potential** — context, register, taboo axis, outsider status — not a global power ranking of swear words and not a traffic light to speak.

| Risk is | Risk is not |
|---------|-------------|
| Hazard note for study | Green / yellow / red permission |
| How easily outsider uptake goes wrong | “Strongest swear” trophy |
| Brake on a recognition companion | Costume license for tourists |
| Often context-heavy | Escalation ladder or dare |

Mild / Low never means tourist-safe, camera-safe, or elder-safe. Mid is research material with brakes on — not “ok if you whisper.” High marks stakes, not a challenge badge. There is no green “safe to perform” band. **Lamps ≠ go.** (Full Low/Mid/High taxonomy: #82; here Risk is one **field job**.)

Risk refuses the story that soft Literal + warm Feels Like equals clearance. Soft glosses and memeable feels can *raise* risk by inviting rehearsal. Knowing the lamp ≠ permission to perform. **Recognition ≠ permission.**

## How the three layers work together

Literal, Feels Like, and Risk are stacked literacy — not three synonyms for “how strong is this insult.”

1. **Literal** = picture (what the words paint).  
2. **Feels Like** = temperature (how uptake often feels on that map).  
3. **Risk** = outsider misfire hazard (how easily *you* go wrong).

Read them as a **culture map**, not a script:

| Misread | Literacy read |
|---------|----------------|
| Soft Literal → soft heat → go | Soft Literal → check Feels Like → check Risk → stay in recognition |
| Surreal picture → harmless meme | Surreal ≠ soft; Risk may still be Mid/High or context-heavy |
| Warm Feels Like → I can join the joke | Warmth inside a repair culture ≠ outsider clearance |
| Low Risk → tourist-safe slang | Low ≠ go; lamps are hazard lights, not crosswalk signals |

**Card anatomy literacy (this piece’s zoom):** refuse Literal-only skims. Register, IPA, and tags matter too (#8 covers full UI order); these three labels are the core trap when English glosses travel alone.
