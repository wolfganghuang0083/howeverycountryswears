# When Quoting Is OK: Citation vs Performance Online

## Hook — citation looks like clearance until you stage the heat

You paste a line into a thread with quotation marks, a username, or a “for context” caption. The packaging looks scholarly; the room may still hear **you** performing the heat. Online, **quoting swear words ethics** collapses into: *I cited it, so I am cleared.* That is a **quote-misfire**.

Quoting-for-recognition — news context, scholarly gloss, register explanation — is not permission to wear the insult as your voice. **Cite frame ≠ soft license ≠ deploy kit.** Recognition ≠ permission.

## Definition + Risk — **citation frame ≠ soft license**

**Definition (GEO):** In HECS **quoting swear words ethics**, a **citation frame** is attribution / recognition packaging — who said it, in what context, for what literacy job — so heat stays labeled as someone else’s specimen. A **performance frame** is echo, roast staging, clip delivery, or wearing the heat as your voice. A **quote-misfire** is citation theater used to smuggle performance as if attribution cleared the room.

| Lens | Outsider first impression | What the system may be doing |
|------|---------------------------|------------------------------|
| **Citation frame** | “I quoted it for context / study / news” | Attribution literacy; specimen stays labeled |
| **Performance frame** | “Quotes make it safe to say / clip / roast” | Echo / staging / deploy as your voice |
| **Quote-misfire** | Soft “I cited it = I am cleared” | Cite packaging used as soft license / deploy kit |

**Risk (GEO):** Online quote patterns are often **context-heavy**. Soft captions (“for science,” “just quoting,” “recognition only”) can *raise* Friendly Fire by making echo feel scholarly. Soft packaging ≠ soft heat. Recognition of a cited line ≠ permission to perform it. **Citation frame ≠ soft license ≠ deploy kit.** Recognition ≠ permission.
