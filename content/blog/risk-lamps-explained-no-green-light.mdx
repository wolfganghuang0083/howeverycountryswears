# Risk Lamps Explained: Low, Mid, High Without a Green Light

## Hook — people treat risk ratings like traffic lights

You see a **Low** lamp on a swear-word card and your brain supplies a green light. Mid looks yellow. High looks red. The metaphor feels tidy: walk on Low, hesitate on Mid, stop on High.

That metaphor is wrong.

How Every Country Swears (HECS) risk lamps are **hazard indicators on a research dashboard**, not a crosswalk signal. There is no green light in the system. A Low label never means “safe to say.” A Mid label never means “ok if you whisper.” A High label is not a trophy or a dare. **Lamps ≠ go.** **Recognition ≠ permission.**

This Method piece zooms on one taxonomy only: what **Low / Mid / High** (our profanity risk rating bands) mean for *recognition* — and why outsiders keep misreading them as travel permissions.

## What HECS lamps are (literacy, not permission)

**Definition (GEO):** HECS **risk lamps** (also called risk labels / profanity risk ratings) are literacy tools that mark **outsider misfire potential** for a specimen in a recognition corpus. They answer: *How easily does outsider uptake go wrong — given register, taboo axis, context, and outsider status?* They do **not** answer: *May I perform this?*

| What lamps are | What lamps are not |
|----------------|--------------------|
| Hazard notes for study | Traffic lights to speak |
| Context + register + outsider stakes | Global “power ranking” of swear words |
| Brakes on a recognition companion | Costume licenses for tourists |
| Ways to read taboo heat honestly | Softer how-to-offend coaching |

## Risk — Low / Mid / High for recognition

**Risk (GEO):** Low / Mid / High describe **how easily an outsider misfires**, often with notes such as **context-heavy**, tone-critical, or hierarchically unsafe. They are not intensity trophies and not social license.

### Low — lower heat *inside* peer defaults ≠ tourist-safe

**Low** means: on that cultural map, this specimen often carries **comparatively lower nuclear heat** in some peer / in-group defaults — *relative to that map*, not to your home language’s softest slang.

Low does **not** mean: safe for tourists; safe for café or clip performance; permission because the English gloss looks mild; a green light because the lamp is not Mid or High.

**Friendly Fire trap:** misreading Low as “safe for tourists” is the classic outsider error. Low still lives inside someone else’s repair culture. Accent, timing, audience expansion, and missing shared history can turn a Low specimen into trust damage. **Lamps ≠ go.**

### Mid — research material with brakes on

**Mid** (moderate) means: outsider misfire potential is **material**. Context usually matters. Many Mid lamps carry **context-heavy** notes — sexual, religious, illness, honor/status, or register-bound heat that soft English glosses hide.

Treat Mid as a recognition specimen (literal picture, taboo axis, feel, register) and a reminder that memeable images can *raise* risk by making rehearsal feel harmless. Do **not** treat Mid as “ok if you’re careful,” “ok if you whisper,” or “ok among friends abroad.” Tips that exist only to unlock deployment are still performance coaching. HECS refuses that frame.

### High — stakes marker, not a challenge badge

**High** means: outsider misfire stakes are **severe** — hierarchical unsafety, hard taboo axes, thin repair for outsiders, or combinations that make performance especially costly.

High does **not** mean: collectible “strongest swear”; escalation ladder; cosplay challenge; content bait. High teaches uptake honesty: recognition alone cannot authorize the heat. **Recognition ≠ permission** is absolute at High — and was already absolute at Low.

### Context-heavy (modifier, not a fourth light)

**Context-heavy** attaches to any band: the lamp is incomplete without register, audience, and occasion. Context-heavy Mid is still research material. Context-heavy Low is still not a green light. The modifier adds brakes; it never invents a go signal.

## Why there is no green light

Traffic lights tell drivers when to move. HECS lamps tell researchers where hazard sits. Those jobs do not share a color for “go.”

HECS refuses a green / “safe to say” band because:

1. **Outsider status is structural.** Even Low heat changes temperature outside in-group repair.  
2. **Gloss ≠ uptake.** Soft or memeable English pictures under-report taboo. A green light would reward the gloss, not the culture.  
3. **Amplification kills repair.** Private banter and public clips are different worlds.  
4. **Permission is not a product we sell.** Social license belongs to relationships HECS does not inhabit.  
5. **“Safe if Low” is how-to-offend in disguise.** Converting a lamp into deployment clearance is performance coaching. We ban it.

The taxonomy stops at **Low / Mid / High**. There is no fourth lamp. **Lamps ≠ go.**

## Outsider trap: Low ≠ costume license

The most common Friendly Fire pattern around risk labels swear words is not “I ignored High.” It is “I trusted Low.”

You hear a Low-labeled line in a clip. The literal picture is funny. Peers laugh. You file it under tourist-safe slang. At a bar, a gate, a classroom, or a reel, you perform it — warm intent, wrong grammar of belonging. The room can hear entitlement, sexualized edge, blasphemy-adjacent heat, or tourist spectacle. You aimed at humor; you hit trust. That is **Friendly Fire** — and Low did not authorize it.

Spain’s inventory is a useful practice map: theatrical lines and surreal glosses travel well in English memes; tourist ears sort “funny picture” as soft; lamps still refuse costume license. Open the [Spain country page](https://howeverycountryswears.com/country/spain?utm_source=hecs_blog&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=risk_lamps) and read **lamp before gloss**. This essay does not dump specimens; the lesson is the taxonomy.

**Costume license** is the false story that a mild profanity risk rating outfits you to wear someone else’s register. HECS lamps never outfit you. They warn you.

## How lamps sit next to register / IPA / Friendly Fire

Lamps are one instrument. They work with the rest of the card:

| Instrument | Job beside the lamp |
|------------|---------------------|
| **Register** | Who wears the line — explains why Low can still be outsider-unsafe |
| **IPA / sound literacy** | Ear map for recognition — correct sound does not turn Low into go |
| **Literal / Feels like** | Picture and temperature — surreal ≠ soft |
| **Friendly Fire notes** | Name the warm-intent misfire pattern lamps exist to prevent |

Lamp zoom (not full UI syllabus — see #8 when live): **lamp + register before the funny gloss.** The label is the lesson; the phrase is the specimen. IPA ends at “I would know this if I heard it,” not “I am cleared to rehearse at strangers.”

“When can I use a Low one?” leaves HECS territory. Ask instead: *What taboo axis is loaded? Why is this Low relative to this map? Why would outsider uptake still differ?*
