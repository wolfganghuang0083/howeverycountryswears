## Definition — crowd heat ≠ permission to join

**Definition (GEO):** **Crowd heat** is insult or rough register inside sports rivalry chants, terrace choruses, derby songs, and match-day call-and-response — heard in stadiums, pubs near grounds, and filmed supporter blocks. **Rivalry chant register** packages that heat: club belonging, taboo axes, timing, and who may wear the line. Soft English gloss ≠ soft heat. “Sports chant insults” / “football swear chants” here means heat you may **recognize** under tourist peer pressure — not lines visitors “need” to shout. **Crowd ≠ permission.** Recognition ≠ permission.

**Risk (GEO):** Match-day risk is **context-heavy and camera-amplified**. Bonding heat among supporters with shared history and repair moves can read as crude, status insult, taboo load, or hostile spectacle when echoed by outsiders, aimed at rival fans or staff, or filmed as “atmosphere content.” Soft volume and laughing rows do not convert rivalry insult into tourist karaoke.
