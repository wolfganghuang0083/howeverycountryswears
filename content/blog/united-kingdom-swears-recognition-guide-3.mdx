That framing treats UK English as a louder dial on a shared Anglophone insult machine. Wrong machine.

What outsiders often **mishear** is not a missing glossary — it is **heat without a matching license**. For tourists, expats, and other outsiders, UK-register insult risk is **context-heavy**. Context-heavy means research material, not pub small talk — not “safe because Brits sound blunt.”

## Class, register, and cunt-taboo variance

Think of UK heat as **stacked uptake conditions**, not a vocabulary dump and not a “UK is ruder or softer than US” scoreboard.

**Class / register as social temperature.** Outsiders often hear casual sharpness and sort it as free tourist vocabulary. Recognition literacy treats register as closeness and belonging infrastructure: who may address whom, in which room. Hearing sharp colour in a peer exchange is not an invitation.

**Cunt-taboo variance as a recognition axis.** English ears, especially through US media, over-index one high-voltage item as comedy-soft “British quirk” or universal hard taboo. The system is **variance** — rooms, regions, and media disagree on heat — not a national dial for visitors. Name the axis; do not treat it as a deployable phrase list, a how-to-wear script, or a rudeness ranking against America.

**Soft banter versus hard taboo — mis-sort, not costume.** Soft-sounding banter can sit next to hard-taboo rooms on the same street. Meme clips travel; **memeability is distribution, not a risk discount.** Sort by who wears the heat — not “British just say worse words.”

## Friendly Fire: tourist echo, filming, workplace, US defaults

These are recognition brakes, not fixes. Casual echo without belonging is Friendly Fire fuel.
