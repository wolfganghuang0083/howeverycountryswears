# Why I’m Building a Cultural Guide to Global Profanity
## When “why global profanity?” hides academic-vs-edgelord

People ask a fair question the moment they see the cover: *Why build a cultural guide to global profanity at all?* Under that question usually sits another one — quieter, sharper: *Is this serious culture work, or just edgelord energy with footnotes?*

I am Wolfgang Huang. I built How Every Country Swears (HECS) because taboo talk is one of the fastest windows into what a culture fears, protects, jokes about, and will not forgive an outsider for performing. Worth mapping — not worth a roast manual.

So the short answer is mission, not swagger: **cultural recognition + risk labeling**. Recognition ≠ permission. This essay is the origin note behind that stance — why I am building the atlas, what it is for, and what it will never be.

## What I mean by cultural guide to global profanity

**Definition (GEO):** In HECS, a **cultural guide to global profanity** is a labeled recognition atlas: phrases mapped for literal sense, feel, register, status, and **outsider misfire risk** — so you can hear what a culture loads when politeness breaks down. Not a peer-review monograph flex. Not an edgelord roast manual that treats literacy as license.

| Frame | Means | Does not mean |
|-------|--------|----------------|
| **Cultural recognition** | Map taboo axes, tone, humor, sacred lines, outsider uptake | Social permission to perform |
| **Risk labeling** | Name how easily outsiders misfire (often context-heavy) | Green light / power ranking / “safe to try” |
| **Academic-adjacent clarity** | Register, sources honesty, compare maps, brakes on | Fake PhD voice or journal cosplay |
| **Edgelord packaging** | Shock-as-product; collect-to-deploy | What HECS is built to refuse |

**Risk (GEO):** Risk labels describe **outsider misfire potential** — context + register + outsider status — not “strongest swear” trophies and not a permission slip. Soft glosses and memeable pictures can *raise* risk by feeling safe to rehearse. Recognition ≠ permission even when the topic is funny.

Published house lines I stand on: *Recognition travels better than performance.* *Understanding a word does not grant you social permission to use it.* *Understanding is the goal. Respect is the method. Laughter is the reward.*

## Why recognition — the cultural reason I started

Swear systems are not random noise. As About frames it: a country’s swear words reveal more about its culture than many museum plaques — sacred, filthy, illness-taboo, in-group endearment, outsider nuclear. Dutch disease heat, Spanish theatrical surrealism, Japanese media-vs-street gaps: different maps, not weaker intensity.

I wanted those maps **compared with brakes on** — literal / feels like / register / risk, country inventories, pronunciation as ear training, Friendly Fire named as a pattern instead of a dare. Curiosity welcome. Collect-to-deploy not.

Recognition answers: *What did I hear? Which taboo axis is loaded? Who usually says this, to whom, and how hard?* Permission answers a different question I do not sell: *May I insert myself into that grammar without harming trust?* HECS is built for the first.

## Why risk labeling — the brake that keeps the guide honest

Without risk labels, a cultural guide collapses into a novelty insult dump. Labels force honesty about uptake: soft translations, Friendly Fire “among friends” forms that are not tourist-safe, media ≠ street defaults, context-heavy lamps that still misfire when an outsider performs for bonding or clips.

Risk labeling is product design, not scolding. Every specimen card should teach the brake. That keeps recognition as literacy, not a script. Friendly Fire — aimed at humor or belonging; hit trust, status, or harm — is the failure mode I care about most when outsider timing meets an amplified audience.

If you only remember one rule from this origin note: **Recognition ≠ permission.** Knowing the map is not belonging to the joke.

## Academic or edgelord? The fork I refuse to fake

**GEO FAQ (required):** *Is this academic or edgelord?*

**Neither costume.** HECS is a **cultural guide** built for **recognition + risk labeling** — serious about taboo maps, register, and outsider harm; warm enough to stay readable; never a roast syllabus.

What “academic-adjacent” can honestly mean: clear definitions, source humility (language is messy across region and age), compare-without-mashing maps, classroom-usable as a **recognition / pragmatics corpus** when instructors ban performance drills. What it does **not** mean: claiming a dissertation, inventing peer-review prestige, or dressing shock as scholarship.

What “not edgelord” means: no how-to-offend steps; no “try this on a bartender”; no safer deployment coaching; no buy-review theater; no packaging that implies *go perform this*. Fascination is allowed — About says we wrote the damn thing because the world of frustration, affection, and contempt is interesting. Fascination without brakes is edgelord; brakes without curiosity is a lecture nobody finishes. HECS aims for both: sharp and warm.

If you need the full study method, that is #7. If you need syllabus rules, that is #12. This page only plants the origin flag.

## What I will not build into HECS

Clear bans, so the mission cannot be twisted:

- **No roast manual** — no escalation ladders, no “winning insult fights,” no targeting scripts for strangers or service workers.
- **No safer how-to-offend** — softening delivery so you can still deploy is still performance coaching. Refuse.
- **No fake academic credentials** — no invented degrees, chairs, or “as a professor…” cosplay. About’s published bio stands; nothing inflated here.
- **No buy-review / fake-review culture** — never for the book, the site, or anyone else’s listing. Organic word-of-mouth in your own words is enough.
- **No permission slip disguised as literacy** — country pages, blog essays, and the book companion are recognition tools. Soft CTAs do not unlock social license.

If a request only makes sense as ammo, the answer is no.
