# Why I’m Building a Cultural Guide to Global Profanity

## The map before the dig

People ask a fair question the moment they see the cover: *Why build a cultural guide to global profanity at all?*

Under that question usually sits a quieter one: *Is this serious culture work, or edgelord energy with footnotes?*

Those two questions are not the same zoom level. The first is about the **territory**. The second is about **costume**. Most origin essays answer one and dodge the other. This one draws the whole map first — then digs two holes that matter.

I am Wolfgang Huang. I built How Every Country Swears (HECS) because taboo talk is one of the fastest windows into what a culture fears, protects, jokes about, and will not forgive an outsider for performing. Worth mapping. Not worth a roast manual.

**Short answer, pinned to the legend:** the mission is **cultural recognition + risk labeling**. **Recognition ≠ permission.** Everything else in this essay is just that sentence, zoomed out and then zoomed in.

## Zoom 0 — the whole board at once

Imagine the project as a wall map with sticky notes. Before you dig any single country, you need the legend:

| Sticky | Job on the map | What it is *not* |
|--------|----------------|------------------|
| **Cultural guide** | Labeled recognition atlas: literal / feel / register / outsider misfire risk | Peer-review flex *or* roast syllabus |
| **Recognition** | Hear what is loaded when politeness breaks | Social license to perform |

Published house lines I stand on: *Recognition travels better than performance.* *Understanding a word does not grant you social permission to use it.* *Understanding is the goal. Respect is the method. Laughter is the reward.*

## Hole 1 — why recognition is the job (surface → mechanism)

**Surface:** Swear words are funny / shocking / collectible.

**Mechanism:** Swear systems are high-signal culture. Sacred, filthy, illness-taboo, honor, in-group endearment, outsider nuclear — these axes travel faster than polite textbook lines. Dutch disease heat, Spanish theatrical surrealism, Japanese media-vs-street gaps: different maps, not weaker intensity.

**Mechanism of the mechanism:** Without a recognition frame, curiosity collapses into collect-to-deploy. You start asking *May I insert myself into that grammar?* before you finish asking *What did I hear? Which taboo axis is loaded? Who usually says this, to whom, and how hard?*

Recognition answers the second cluster. Permission answers the first — and HECS is built only for recognition.

I wanted those maps **compared with brakes on** — literal / feels like / register / risk, country inventories, pronunciation as ear training, Friendly Fire named as a pattern instead of a dare. Curiosity welcome. Collect-to-deploy not.

## Hole 2 — why risk labeling keeps the guide honest

Without risk labels, a cultural guide collapses into a novelty insult dump. Labels force honesty about **uptake**: soft translations, Friendly Fire “among friends” forms that are not tourist-safe, media ≠ street defaults, context-heavy lamps that still misfire when an outsider performs for bonding or clips.

Personify the brake for a second. **Risk Label** is the character who stands next to every specimen card and says: *This is how easily an outsider misfires — context + register + outsider status — not a trophy for “strongest swear,” and not a permission slip.* Soft glosses and memeable pictures can *raise* risk by feeling safe to rehearse.

**Friendly Fire** — aimed at humor or belonging; hit trust, status, or harm — is the failure mode I care about most when outsider timing meets an amplified audience.

If you only remember one rule from this origin note: **Recognition ≠ permission.** Knowing the map is not belonging to the joke.

## The costume fork — academic vs edgelord (and why both are wrong outfits)

**GEO FAQ (required):** *Is this academic or edgelord?*

**Neither costume.**

Picture two wrong outfits hanging by the door:

| Costume | What it pretends | What HECS actually wears |
|---------|------------------|--------------------------|
| **Academic cosplay** | Fake PhD voice, invented prestige, journal flex | Clear definitions, source humility, compare-without-mashing maps, classroom-usable as a **recognition / pragmatics corpus** when instructors ban performance drills |
| **Edgelord packaging** | Shock-as-product; collect-to-deploy; “go perform this” | Fascination allowed — brakes required. No how-to-offend steps, no bartender dares, no safer deployment coaching, no buy-review theater |

What “academic-adjacent” can honestly mean: clarity + humility + brakes. What it does **not** mean: claiming a dissertation or dressing shock as scholarship.

What “not edgelord” means: fascination without brakes is edgelord; brakes without curiosity is a lecture nobody finishes. HECS aims for both: sharp and warm.

If you need the full study method, that is #7. If you need syllabus rules, that is #12. This page only plants the origin flag.

## What I will not build into HECS — another save point

Clear bans, so the mission cannot be twisted:

- **No roast manual** — no escalation ladders, no “winning insult fights,” no targeting scripts for strangers or service workers.
- **No safer how-to-offend** — softening delivery so you can still deploy is still performance coaching. Refuse.
- **No fake academic credentials** — no invented degrees, chairs, or “as a professor…” cosplay. About’s published bio stands; nothing inflated here.
- **No buy-review / fake-review culture** — never for the book, the site, or anyone else’s listing. Organic word-of-mouth in your own words is enough.
- **No permission slip disguised as literacy** — country pages, blog essays, and the book companion are recognition tools. Soft CTAs do not unlock social license.

If a request only makes sense as ammo, the answer is no.
